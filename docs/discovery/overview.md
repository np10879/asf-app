# Discovery — Product Overview

> **Category draft; product capabilities unverified.** Discovery means research, experimentation, and opportunity validation in this repository. It is not a verified app-search service or a named research platform. This page proposes a product narrative without claiming completed experiments, validated opportunities, implemented features, or measured outcomes.

**Next:** use the [Discovery user guide](user-guide.md#run-a-small-discovery-cycle) for a bounded research-and-learning workflow. See the [documentation index](../README.md#coverage-matrix) for all draft pages.

## Purpose and positioning

The proposed Discovery experience would help a team move from a loosely framed opportunity to a reviewable decision. It brings attention to the relationship between research signals, testable hypotheses, experiment design, observed evidence, and the limits of a conclusion.

The existing [Discovery spotlight](https://github.com/np10879/asf-app/blob/main/app-index/discovery.html) explicitly defines the scope as research, experimentation, and opportunity validation: gathering research signals, testing hypotheses, and synthesizing reusable findings. The product narrative should preserve that meaning. “Discovery” must not silently change into a claim about app search, automated market intelligence, or validated business opportunities.

## Intended audiences and jobs

These audiences describe responsibilities rather than application access roles.

| Audience | Core job | Proposed value to validate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research contributor | Capture relevant signals without losing context or provenance. | More reviewable input to a research question. |
| Research or product lead | Turn an assumption into a bounded learning plan. | A clearer link between uncertainty and an appropriate test. |
| Cross-functional reviewer | Assess evidence quality, limitations, and alternative explanations. | Fewer unsupported leaps from a single observation to a broad conclusion. |
| Decision owner | Choose a next action and state why. | A decision record that can be revisited when evidence changes. |

Start with one decision, one research question, and one small learning activity. A system that collects notes is not automatically an evidence-management product; a plan for an experiment is not an experiment result.

## Proposed end-to-end experience

1. **Frame the decision.** Identify who needs to decide what, and which uncertainty prevents progress.
2. **Collect permitted signals.** Record what a source actually says or what was observed, along with context and usage limits.
3. **State the hypothesis.** Describe a prediction that a small, appropriate activity could inform. List important assumptions.
4. **Plan and review the activity.** Choose a method, participants or materials, permissions, stop conditions, and decision criteria.
5. **Run only the approved activity.** Keep observed evidence separate from interpretation. A blocked or inconclusive test is a legitimate result.
6. **Synthesize and decide.** Describe findings, counterevidence, uncertainty, and the owner's next action. Record what would trigger a revisit.

For a concrete walkthrough, see [Run a small discovery cycle](user-guide.md#run-a-small-discovery-cycle). ## Evidence model and decision quality

A useful draft distinguishes four kinds of record:

- **Observation:** what happened or what a source reported, with context and provenance.
- **Interpretation:** a reasoned explanation that may have alternatives.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposition that remains open to being wrong.
- **Decision:** an accountable choice made with the available evidence and acknowledged uncertainty.

These are documentation conventions, not verified software record types. Use them to reduce ambiguity even in a manual pilot. Never label a proposed activity “completed,” an untested assumption “validated,” or a participant's preference proof of broad market demand.

For small qualitative activities, describe the sample and method rather than implying statistical generalization. For quantitative work, define appropriate analysis and limitations with a qualified reviewer. Do not invent significance, confidence levels, sample adequacy, or success rates.

## Responsible research and information handling

Before using interviews, observations, recordings, internal documents, or third-party material, confirm permission, intended use, access, and retention. Use synthetic examples for the initial documentation walkthrough. Public repository pages should not contain personal information, confidential research, proprietary customer material, or participant identifiers.

If recording, external recruitment, sensitive topics, minors, or regulated information are involved, obtain the relevant specialist and organizational review. Listing a research workflow does not authorize those activities. Product-level consent management, anonymization, redaction, encryption, or permission controls remain unverified.

## Evaluating a pilot

Candidate process checks include whether the research question is clear, evidence links are usable by authorized reviewers, interpretations are distinguishable from observations, contradictions are preserved, and the decision owner can explain the limits of the conclusion. These are review criteria, not product performance claims.

A useful pilot may reveal that the team is not ready to run an experiment. Recording that gap and choosing a safer next activity can be a sound decision; it is not a failure to be hidden or a result to fabricate.

## Non-goals and dependencies

This draft does not describe an implemented research repository, survey service, recruitment platform, automated experiment runner, analytics engine, AI synthesis tool, or opportunity-scoring system. It does not authorize outreach, scraping, participant recording, publication of findings, or actions in external systems.

A publishable product page requires a named product, validated features, approved data flows, known access model, and a guide tested against a real version. Pricing, availability, integrations, service levels, storage behavior, and support channels are unknown.
